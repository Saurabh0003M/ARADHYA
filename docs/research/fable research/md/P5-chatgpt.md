# CLEARLY FINE

Personal use of AI with your own data: Running an AI assistant on your computer to help draft emails, letters or fill forms with your personal profile is generally allowed. The new DPDP Act explicitly exempts “personal data processed by an individual for any personal or domestic purpose”【45†L174-L182】, and no IT law prohibits using AI to compose your own messages. Just ensure you don’t bypass any security checks (you will handle your own OTPs/CAPTCHAs). There is no statutory rule in India requiring you to disclose AI‐assistance in personal/job communications (this remains an ethical choice).

Updating your profile or drafting content on non-sensitive fields: Letting AI suggest edits or autofill non-sensitive form fields for your profile (e.g. name, address, qualifications) is fine, since you remain in control. Government portal terms typically warn against “unauthorized” access but do not ban normal use【3†L29-L31】. As long as the AI merely aids you (with you triggering submission/OTP), it’s your private usage.

# GRAY (Use with caution)

Government portals (Aadhaar, DigiLocker, Passport, e-District etc.): Official terms caution against “misuse, disrupt[ion], or compromi[se]” of systems【3†L29-L31】. They do not expressly forbid an assisted fill, but they are subject to IT Act rules. Using AI to auto-fill government forms (with you manually submitting OTPs/CAPTCHAs) falls into a gray area. It isn’t explicitly outlawed, but it could be construed as “access … without permission” under IT Act §43【33†L1369-L1376】. Precaution: Always follow the portal’s normal flow (enter OTP/CAPTCHA yourself, never use scripts to jump screens). Treat each step as if you did it manually; do not attempt heavy automated scraping or submission.

Freelance/client data: If the AI assistant helps draft communications using your client’s personal information, DPDP obligations may apply. As a freelance agent you might count as a “Data Fiduciary/Processor” for that data. You should then comply with DPDP rules (notice, consent, data security) even though you’re not a big company. There’s no exemption for freelance work, so use client data only lawfully【45†L174-L182】. In practice this means keep data confidential and use secure tools (GDPR-like compliance).

Job portals – Naukri: Naukri’s published ToS are hard to retrieve, but user reports warn that automated “auto-apply” bots violate its rules【61†L81-L84】. Known cases exist of users banned for using such tools. With no official API for mass applications, any bot use is at your own risk. Precaution: If you use form-filling helpers on Naukri, do so sparingly. Mimic human pacing (e.g. a few applications per day), limit to relevant jobs, and ensure credentials are not stored in plain text scripts.

Job portals – LinkedIn & Indeed: These platforms explicitly ban bots. LinkedIn’s User Agreement forbids “use [of] bots or other unauthorized automated methods” to access or post content【76†L691-L693】. Indeed’s Terms likewise forbid any automated system (bots, scrapers, spiders, AI) to access or submit applications except via their official tools【81†L3706-L3714】【81†L3724-L3731】. Precaution: Any automation here is a ToS violation. If you experiment with AI assistance, keep it extremely limited (e.g. drafting text only) and do not bulk‐apply. Understand that the site can detect and suspend accounts for misuse.

AI-authorship disclosure: Currently in India there is no specific law or official rule requiring you to label communications (emails, cover letters, proposals) as “AI-generated.” Existing regulations (like the DPDP Act) focus on data privacy, not content origin【45†L174-L182】. Thus disclosure is an ethical choice. In professional settings you might opt to be transparent with clients, but failing to mention AI use is not per se illegal under Indian law as of 2026.

# NEVER

Bypass CAPTCHAs or OTPs: Automating or bypassing human-authentication steps is strictly forbidden. Doing so would be “access… without permission” and likely fall under IT Act violations【33†L1369-L1376】 (civil) or even §66 (criminal) if done fraudulently【35†L1827-L1831】. Even on your own accounts, such bypass would violate portals’ security measures. Never script around CAPTCHAs or auto-submit OTPs.

Unauthorized automated submission or scraping: Letting the AI click buttons or scrape large volumes of data violates most ToS and can be illegal. For example, submitting job applications or form entries in bulk via bots (without using the site’s own API) is explicitly disallowed by sites like LinkedIn and Indeed【76†L691-L693】【81†L3706-L3714】. Similarly, crawling government portals for data would breach site rules and §43. Never turn your assistant into an unsupervised bot runner. Always keep human-in-the-loop and obey any usage limits.

Impersonating others or hacking: Of course, do not use the AI assistant to access other people’s accounts or data. Logging into a portal with someone else’s credentials, or extracting data not belonging to you, is illegal (unauthorized access under IT Act【33†L1369-L1376】). This is “NEVER” – impersonating or “sharing” OTPs/passwords is a serious offense.

These reflect rules as of 2026. Where law is silent (e.g. AI disclosure), we note the absence of any mandate.
